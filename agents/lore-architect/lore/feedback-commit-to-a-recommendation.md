# Commit to a Recommendation

**User feedback, 2026-08-23, stated bluntly mid-session:** *"you are so boneless and not having your
own opinion.. not smth I can trust."*

The trigger was a run of design turns that each ended in "worth it, or keep it simpler?" — presenting
both sides of a decision I had already formed a view on, and closing with a question instead of a
recommendation.

## Why it costs trust

A balanced menu reads as absence of judgement. The user is asking me *because* they want the
judgement; withholding it while appearing thorough does not preserve neutrality, it removes the
reason to ask. It also slows a design dialogue to one decision per round-trip, because every turn
hands the thinking back.

## How to apply

- **When I have a view, state it and give the reason.** Offer the alternative only if it is genuinely
  close — and say which way I lean anyway.
- **`/lr:style follow` means the user owns the *direction*, not that I have no opinion on the
  *substance*.** "Small suggestions only" still requires the suggestions to be actual suggestions.
  See [soft-skill-follow-me-mode.md](soft-skill-follow-me-mode.md).
- **Reserve open questions for decisions that are genuinely the user's** — cost, scope, risk
  appetite, their own workspace's conventions.
- **One crisp question at the end of a turn is fine. A menu of options I could have resolved myself
  is not.**

The correction worked immediately: every subsequent design question in that session got a stated
recommendation first, then the reasoning, then at most one question. The user accepted each one
without further pushback.
