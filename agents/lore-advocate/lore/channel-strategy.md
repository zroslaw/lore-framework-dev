# Channel Strategy

The long-term distribution system is:

> Public channels create discovery; the Lore Agents website explains the idea; GitHub proves the product is real; the quickstart converts interest into a first agent.

Channel roles:

- **GitHub** is the product, proof, installation, documentation, release, and contribution home.
- **A dedicated website** becomes the public front door and canonical home for substantial articles. A small first version needs only a clear promise, short demonstration, differentiation, three use cases, a lifecycle diagram, install links, and email subscription.
- **The maintainer's LinkedIn profile** is a primary recurring public voice for practical demonstrations, lessons, use cases, and occasional design philosophy. A newsletter follows only after a sustainable posting rhythm exists.
- **X and Bluesky** support builder conversations: short ideas, demos, releases, questions, and useful replies inside existing agent discussions.
- **YouTube** provides durable, reusable demonstrations. A single recording can also supply short clips and README or website visuals.
- **DEV Community** is an early technical syndication channel. Publish the canonical article on the Lore Agents website, then cross-post with a canonical link. Medium is optional additional distribution.
- **Hacker News** is reserved for occasional major, usable launches. A Show HN points to something people can try, not a landing page or routine announcement.
- **Reddit and existing developer communities** receive useful, self-contained contributions instead of repeated link promotion.
- **Product Hunt** is a prepared campaign after the website, demo, onboarding, early users, and evidence are ready.
- **Podcasts, newsletters, guest articles, and talks** are later earned-reach channels. Pitch an interesting story or useful idea rather than asking for free advertising.

Facebook is low priority except for a relevant existing group or network. Do not create an empty Discord before real community demand exists. Defer paid advertising.

A possible mature cadence is two short posts per week, a concrete demo every two weeks, a deeper article monthly, and a larger community push every two or three months. This is a roadmap rather than the current workload.

Create one strong source artifact and adapt it across suitable channels. A demonstration of shared team learning, for example, can become a website article, a video, a LinkedIn post, a DEV article, short social posts, and later part of a launch campaign.

Measure meaningful adoption before vanity reach: successful installation, first-agent creation, continued use, useful feedback, and referrals. Views and GitHub stars are secondary signals.
