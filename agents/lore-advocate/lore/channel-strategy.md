# Channel Strategy

The long-term distribution system is:

> Public channels create discovery; the Lore Agents website explains the idea; GitHub proves the product is real; the quickstart converts interest into a first agent.

Order the effort by measured leverage, not by channel count. Across the two projects studied, **no content channel moved either curve**; installation reach and framework integrations moved both. Content still does necessary work — it explains, persuades, and earns credibility — but treat it as the message, not the growth mechanism. See [growth evidence](growth-evidence.md).

Channel roles:

- **GitHub** is the product, proof, installation, documentation, release, and contribution home.
- **Plugin directories on the coding-agent hosts** are a distribution channel in their own right, and the highest-leverage one available. Reducing install friction where our market already lives is the only move measured to produce a step change that held; see [growth evidence](growth-evidence.md).
- **Other people's agent frameworks and toolchains** are a channel, not just an integration concern. A pull request that makes Lore Agents available from inside a framework a developer already installed is the cheapest compounding distribution found in the sweep.
- **A dedicated website** becomes the public front door and canonical home for substantial articles. A small first version needs only a clear promise, short demonstration, differentiation, three use cases, a lifecycle diagram, install links, and email subscription.
- **The maintainer's LinkedIn profile** is a primary recurring public voice for practical demonstrations, lessons, use cases, and occasional design philosophy. A newsletter follows only after a sustainable posting rhythm exists.
- **X and Bluesky** support builder conversations: short ideas, demos, releases, questions, and useful replies inside existing agent discussions.
- **YouTube** provides durable, reusable demonstrations. A single recording can also supply short clips and README or website visuals.
- **DEV Community** was treated as an early technical syndication channel; measured evidence now says it does not work in this category. claude-mem drew one third-party article with zero reactions twelve months late, and mem0 drew fifteen articles peaking at two reactions on a 65K-star project. Cross-post there only as a near-free byproduct of an article written for somewhere else, with a canonical link, and expect nothing from it. Medium is optional additional distribution on the same terms.
- **Hacker News** remains reserved for occasional major, usable launches, and a Show HN must point to something people can try. The evidence adds a limit: post it, but do not build a launch around it. claude-mem self-submitted for 2 points; mem0's Show HN scored 201 points and 61 comments, and still produced fewer stars over five days than a quiet week with no launch at all.
- **Reddit and existing developer communities** receive useful, self-contained contributions instead of repeated link promotion. Reddit is unmeasured rather than dismissed — the research sweep was blocked from it, so its value in this category is an open question, not a low number.
- **Product Hunt** is a prepared campaign after the website, demo, onboarding, early users, and evidence are ready.
- **Podcasts, newsletters, guest articles, and talks** are later earned-reach channels. Pitch an interesting story or useful idea rather than asking for free advertising.

Facebook is low priority except for a relevant existing group or network. Do not create an empty Discord before real community demand exists. Defer paid advertising.

A possible mature cadence is two short posts per week, a concrete demo every two weeks, a deeper article monthly, and a larger community push every two or three months. This is a roadmap rather than the current workload.

Create one strong source artifact and adapt it across suitable channels. A demonstration of shared team learning, for example, can become a website article, a video, a LinkedIn post, short social posts, and later part of a launch campaign. Adaptation is close to free, so weak channels can still receive a copy; what the evidence forbids is *planning* growth around them.

Measure meaningful adoption before vanity reach: successful installation, first-agent creation, continued use, useful feedback, and referrals. Views and GitHub stars are lagging attention metrics, not adoption — the measurement discipline and the sources that hold up are in [growth evidence](growth-evidence.md).
